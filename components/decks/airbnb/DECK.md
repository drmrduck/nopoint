# AirBed&Breakfast — 2009 seed deck (faithful recreation)

This is a tribute recreation of the original Airbnb (then "AirBed&Breakfast") seed
pitch deck shared by Brian Chesky in 2009. It is reproduced here as a teaching
example of a tight, narrative-led pitch — not as financial advice, and not the
property of the current operator.

## Source material

The deck circulated publicly via Brian Chesky's LinkedIn / Medium and is widely
cited in pitch deck collections (Slidebean, Pitch.com, etc.). All numbers are as
they appear in the original 2009 deck. The 2008 booking-volume figures, the
$200M projected revenue, and the $500K ask are all part of the historical record
— they reflect 2009-era reasoning, not present-day Airbnb economics.

## Design rules

- **Cream background, ink text, Rausch red accent.** The original deck used a
    near-white cream paper feel with a single warm red as the only accent. We
    keep that palette: bg `#FAF6F0`, text `#1B1A1B`, accent `#FF5A5F` (modern
    Rausch — the original 2009 red was slightly more coral but Rausch is the
    legible signature). No gradients, no glow, no drop shadows.
- **Big black numbers.** Market validation, market size, business model, and
    financials lean on a single number per slide rendered at hero size with a
    short caption. Don't dilute by adding bar charts.
- **Headline at top, content below.** Every slide reads top-to-bottom: a 1-line
    headline, then the substance. No body-copy paragraphs.
- **No founder ornament.** Team slide is three faces, three names, three roles —
    no LinkedIn URLs, no certifications.

## Slide plan

1. Cover — name + tagline
2. Problem — three pains
3. Solution — three benefits, mirrored to the problems
4. Market Validation — Couchsurfing + Craigslist evidence
5. Market Size — 1.9B / 532M / 10.6M funnel
6. Product — search → list → book
7. Business Model — 10% commission math
8. Market Adoption — event-triggered launches (SXSW, DNC)
9. Competition — 2x2 quadrant
10. Competitive Advantages — comparison matrix
11. Team — three founders
12. Financials — three-year revenue projection
13. The Ask — $500K, runway, use of funds

## What we did NOT change

- The numbers. The 2008-era booking-volume figures and the $200M Year-3 revenue
    projection are quoted as-is — including the optimistic ones. That's part of
    the lesson: a deck that closed funding doesn't have to be perfectly hedged.
- The slide order. The narrative arc (problem → solution → validation → market
    → product → model → traction → competition → why-us → team → numbers → ask)
    is what made this deck a teaching standard. We preserve it.
